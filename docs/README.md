# OctoAcme Project Management Docs

This repository contains the core project management guidance used by OctoAcme. The process is designed to help cross-functional teams move from idea to delivery in a structured, transparent, and measurable way. The lifecycle begins with initiation to validate business value and define success, continues through planning and execution, and closes with release and continuous improvement. Across all phases, the team emphasizes clear ownership, stakeholder alignment, and iterative delivery of small, testable increments.

## Project management lifecycle

OctoAcme’s model follows a repeatable lifecycle:

1. Initiation: confirm the problem, define objectives, identify stakeholders, and decide whether to move forward.
2. Planning: create a backlog, timelines, milestones, dependencies, and acceptance criteria.
3. Execution and tracking: run the team rhythm, monitor progress, escalate blockers, and maintain quality through reviews and CI.
4. Release and deployment: validate readiness, deploy with a rollback plan, and communicate outcomes to stakeholders.
5. Close and continuous improvement: capture lessons learned, document action items, and improve the process over time.

## Roles and responsibilities

The process relies on clear roles across the team. Project Managers coordinate delivery, schedules, risks, and documentation. Product Managers define the customer problem, prioritize work, and measure outcome success. Developers build and test the work, QA validates quality and acceptance criteria, and stakeholders provide inputs, approvals, and business context. These roles work together to reduce ambiguity, improve accountability, and keep delivery aligned with customer value.

## Communication and quality practices

OctoAcme uses a structured communication rhythm to keep teams and stakeholders informed. This includes daily or frequent working syncs, weekly PM/Product updates, demos, milestone reviews, and escalation paths for blockers or risks. The team also maintains a single source of truth for status, risks, and decisions, with regular review of dependencies and escalation when issues affect delivery or customer impact.

Quality is built into each stage of delivery. The process includes small PRs, CI validation, security scanning, testing, acceptance criteria, and manual QA where needed. Releases require smoke tests, rollback planning, stakeholder communication, and post-deployment verification, while retrospectives convert lessons into action items that feed future improvements. This combination of disciplined communication, shared accountability, and quality gates helps the team deliver reliably while continuing to learn and improve.
